Critical finding: location is always on

Always-on GPS plus background tracking can reveal a person's home address and daily routine, including when they are away. This conflicts with data minimization (GDPR Article 5(1)(c)) and the course ethics standard 5.3.4 cited in this activity.

Computer laws: data protection

The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) sets rules for handling personal data and protecting people’s privacy. It requires data to be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently, for specified purposes, and limited to what is necessary. Personal data should not be kept longer than needed, and organizations must protect it with appropriate security and be able to demonstrate compliance. The GDPR also expects privacy protections to be built into systems and enabled by default, rather than added only after problems arise.

Ethics in computing

The ACM Code of Ethics asks computing professionals to contribute to society and human well-being, avoid harm, and be honest and trustworthy. It also emphasizes fairness, respect for privacy, and honoring confidentiality. In practice, an ethical developer considers how a system may affect people, collects only information that serves a clear purpose, and protects that information from misuse. Ethical responsibility applies even when a particular action is not explicitly prohibited by law.

Sources

  • ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

    The ACM Code describes responsibilities for computing professionals, including contributing to society and avoiding harm. It calls for honesty, fairness, respect for privacy, and protection of confidential information. It also encourages professionals to consider the consequences of their work and prioritize the public good.

    Read the ACM Code of Ethics
  • General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

    The GDPR is the European Union’s regulation for protecting personal data and establishing rights and responsibilities around its use. Its core principles include lawful and transparent processing, purpose limitation, data minimization, storage limitation, and security. It also requires organizations to demonstrate accountability and consider data protection in system design and default settings.
